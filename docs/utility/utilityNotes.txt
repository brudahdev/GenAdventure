Yes, in the context of that book, those would be called factors.

More academically, they would usually be called attributes, criteria, or features depending on the field.

For example, an attack decision might look like this:

Action candidates:

Attack with sword
Attack with bow
Use spell

Decision factors (attributes/criteria):

Damage dealt
Chance to hit
Distance from target
Resource cost

Each factor describes some aspect of an action.

A subtle distinction:

Damage dealt (raw game value) is an attribute.
"How desirable is this amount of damage?" is a utility function applied to that attribute.
The final score for the attack choice is the overall utility.

So:

Action
 ├── Attribute: Damage dealt
 │       └── Utility value: 0.8
 │
 ├── Attribute: Chance to hit
 │       └── Utility value: 0.9
 │
 └── Combined
         └── Action utility: 0.85

The book compresses the middle step and often calls both the factor score and final score "utility," which is convenient for implementation but less precise academically.
